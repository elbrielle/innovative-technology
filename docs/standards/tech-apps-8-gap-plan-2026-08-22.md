# Tech Apps 8 / iCEV Gap-and-Fold Plan

Research date: 2026-08-22  
Standards basis: 19 TAC §126.19, Technology Applications, Grade 8, Adopted 2022  
Course basis: Canvas `23402`, 34-module snapshot generated 2026-08-21

## Executive finding

The selected public iCEV curriculum is **Digital Literacy for 8th Grade / Technology Applications for 8th Grade**. iCEV's Texas TIA surface lists the corresponding assessment as **Tech Apps 8th (Proc24)**. The safe operating assumption is now **pretest/posttest access only**. Full course or lesson access would be useful, but no VILS bridge may depend on it until the live district/iCEV surface proves that teachers receive instructional content as well as the assessments.

The VILS course is strong in design process, multimedia creation, programming revision, robotics, AI/data investigation, research, cybersecurity, troubleshooting, and project communication. The largest risk is not the ambitious project work. It is the explicit practical and foundational material that a broad Tech Apps assessment can sample: pseudocode, named variables and data types, text-based nested loops, keyboarding growth, spreadsheets, file/storage choices, application shortcuts, digital footprint, intellectual property, cyberbullying scenarios, and technology history/trends.

## Evidence boundary

The dated 2026-08-19 module audit names 19 of the 38 official Grade 8 expectations (36 lettered expectations plus whole expectations (6) and (7)). The current snapshot changed after that audit, so its 19 unnamed expectations are a discovery queue, not a final noncoverage verdict.

A current student-page search found no explicit student evidence for:

- pseudocode;
- text-based nested loops;
- named variables with multiple data types and operations;
- WPM/accuracy keyboarding growth;
- digital footprint;
- intellectual-property/copyright law;
- technology history/global-trend evaluation.

It found thin, indirect, or optional evidence for:

- file management (strongest in Piskel source/export naming, but not the full storage hierarchy);
- shortcuts (isolated `Ctrl+C` and `Ctrl+Z`, not independent selection/use across applications);
- cyberbullying (an optional Capstone topic/resource, not a required warning-sign case study);
- spreadsheets (Robot Salad is enrichment and its current student page is only a short worksheet direction);
- data publication (several strong data projects, but an audience-facing data product is not consistently required);
- local versus remote storage selection.

## What the iCEV course signals

The official public 8th-grade course list includes four-unit sequences for Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides plus Fundamentals of Computational Thinking, Data, Digital Citizenship, File Management & Cloud Computing, Keyboarding, Programming, Using Technology, Cyber Threats, and Technology: Past, Present & Future.

An iCEV public Grade 8 correlation/resource PDF makes several expectations especially concrete:

- a pseudocode project analyzes patterns, variables, abstraction, and testing;
- a programming project requires nested loops and named variables using multiple data types;
- a public-service announcement focuses on monitoring digital footprints;
- a keyboarding drill records before/after WPM using correct technique;
- a citation activity uses multiple digital-media forms and intellectual-property attribution.

These examples should inform skill/evidence design even if only the assessments are provided. Do not copy vendor materials into VILS; build original lessons around the official TEKS and the existing VILS narrative. If the district later receives licensed iCEV lessons, evaluate and fold them in selectively rather than making them a prerequisite.

## Instructional sourcing ladder

Use the first source that provides accurate, accessible, sufficiently deep student evidence:

1. active VILS work already in the compressed course;
2. Verizon's 2026 Default Term course materials;
3. archived VILS material worth rebuilding, including the AIR pseudocode lesson;
4. district-approved external or open educational resources;
5. original VILS authoring.

The [Default Term source audit](verizon-default-term-gap-source-audit-2026-08-22.md) records what the refreshed Verizon courses actually contribute. They are a source bank, not an automatic quality stamp.

## Do not overteach the application suites

The public iCEV course contains four units each for Docs, Sheets, and Slides, but teachers may receive only the pretest and posttest. Even if the lessons are provided, VILS should not automatically insert twelve full vendor units. Students already use documents, presentations, media, and publishing tools throughout the course. The efficient approach is:

1. verify the exact test/playlist and available standard-level reporting;
2. use pretest or a local skills check to distinguish vocabulary/interface gaps from production skill;
3. assign short source-vetted or original just-in-time labs only for the missing skill; use iCEV content only when licensed access is confirmed;
4. immediately apply the skill in a VILS project;
5. retain one observable artifact or performance as evidence.

Examples:

- A student who can format and share a project document but cannot identify interface controls needs a short interface/shortcut lab, not four Docs units.
- A student who has never built a formula and chart needs an explicit Sheets sequence before the SPIKE data product.
- A student who already presents and publishes effectively can demonstrate Slides proficiency through the existing pitch/capstone route.

## Proposed pacing architecture

### Thin recurring spine

- **Keyboarding:** a teacher-selectable sequence of mini-lessons, baseline, recurring short practice, midpoint, and final. Provide cadence templates, not a mandatory calendar, plus optional full-period launch/remediation routes.
- **File/shortcut routine:** use the same naming, location, backup, conversion, and shortcut language every time a new tool appears.
- **Tool-transfer card:** “What is familiar? What is different? Which shortcut/storage choice transfers?”

### Folded additions

- Intro to CS Days 1–3: structured pseudocode before blocks, pattern/variable annotation, and revision after testing.
- Video Game Design/RVR: reuse the same planning artifact for abstraction, feature planning, collaborative roles/timeline, literal execution, and iteration; use Permission to Come Aboard only as an optional source/scaffold.
- Graphic Design: technology history/trends, intellectual property, digital footprint.
- SPIKE: Sheets, data types, formulas, charts, analysis, prediction, published recommendation.
- Capstone/portfolio: source/license record, storage/retrieval proof, final data or evidence communication.

### Explicit lessons that should not be hidden inside another unit

- 1–2 day text-based nested-loop bridge after block-code experience;
- 1-day cyberbullying scenario lesson;
- initial file-management setup and measures;
- targeted Sheets fundamentals when the diagnostic shows students cannot build formulas/charts independently.

Keyboarding is a curriculum kit rather than a fixed unit: mini-lessons and benchmarks recur, while teachers may choose a full-period technique launch or remediation clinic when their students need it.

## Cross-lane opportunity

The licensed Delightex AR + VR route currently budgets about 15 periods. The proposed unlicensed Interactive Experience Studio targets 8–10 periods. At legacy campuses, protect the reclaimed time for the Tech Apps gaps rather than adding unrelated enrichment:

- 1–2 periods text-code/nested loops;
- 1 period spreadsheet fundamentals or reinforcement;
- 1 period digital footprint/intellectual property;
- 1 period cyberbullying or technology-trends work;
- keyboarding remains a flexible recurring strand, with optional full-period launch/remediation when locally useful.

Licensed campuses still need the same TEKS bridge. Their schedule will require compression elsewhere or the same micro-lessons embedded in existing project days; license status must not create different tested-standard access.

## Required verification before authoring

- live iCEV assessment title assigned to the tested teachers and a separate yes/no inventory of instructional course access;
- Tech Apps 8th (Proc24) assessment window and growth rules;
- whether reports expose item, standard, or strand performance;
- current VILS snapshot and a regenerated 38-expectation evidence audit;
- campus calendar and actual instructional minutes;
- approved text-code, keyboarding, and spreadsheet platforms;
- accommodations and EB-access route for timed keyboarding and terminology-heavy checks.
