## Bottom-line quality judgment

The refreshed courses are useful as a **source bank**, not as drop-in high-quality replacements for the compressed VILS course.

Strengths:

- short, predictable 1–2 period lesson shapes;
- clear introductory vocabulary and teacher-facing timing;
- usable MakeCode, sensor, robotics, technology-history, and immersive-media ideas;
- linked facilitator guides, presentations, checkpoints, and self-paced modules;
- several activities can be adapted without changing the current VILS narrative arc.

Limitations:

- much of the teacher guidance is generic “continue through the interactive module” language;
- the scope uses CSTA/ISTE rather than Texas Grade 8 TEKS and does not prove assessed student evidence;
- some scope rows have objective/overview mismatches, editing errors, and repeated generic sustainability language;
- several useful requirements are optional enrichment rather than required artifacts;
- the refreshed scopes do not supply keyboarding, spreadsheets, file management, shortcuts, digital footprint, intellectual property, cyberbullying case analysis, pseudocode, or text-based nested loops.

## Pseudocode retrofit target

The archived AIR lesson is a good optional first 20–30 minutes, not a complete Grade 8 lesson and not necessarily a new standalone unit. The active Intro to CS sequence already provides the stronger home: Day 1 moves from an ordinary-language route to Code.org blocks, Day 2 identifies patterns/loops/events, and Day 3 predicts, tests, and revises with a bug log. The rebuilt product should be a reusable **Pseudocode Passport** folded into those lessons, Video Game Design, and RVR. Across the sequence, students should:

1. decompose the dock problem into navigation, pickup, carrying state, and delivery subproblems;
2. find repeated movement patterns;
3. name variables such as `position`, `boxesRemaining`, and `carryingBox`;
4. abstract repeated moves into a reusable procedure;
5. create a collaborative pseudocode plan with roles and a short development timeline;
6. have another team execute the instructions literally;
7. record the failure, revise the pseudocode, and explain the improvement;
8. translate one section into executable block code, then complete a separate text-based nested-loop bridge.

This preserves the familiar AIR narrative and recorded video as optional source material while avoiding a duplicate coding unit. The passport and existing coding work can meet §126.19(c)(1)(A)–(F); §126.19(c)(2)(B) still requires explicit text-based nested-loop evidence.

## Keyboarding platform shortlist

No platform was found in the active VILS course, the Default Term scopes, the Verizon allowlist, local historical files, or a focused recent-browser-history check. The prior app remains unidentified.

### 1. Typing.com — leading pilot

Why it fits:

- free teacher portal with Grade 8 lessons, tests, games, WPM/accuracy reporting, and improvement-over-time reports;
- unlimited students, classes, teachers, and schools advertised for the teacher portal;
- student email is optional; Google, Google Classroom, Clever, ClassLink, Microsoft, and Schoology integrations are advertised;
- suitable for recurring self-paced practice and formal benchmark tests.

Pilot constraints:

- the free edition is ad-supported;
- the current privacy policy says the free version uses Google Analytics and an advertising partner for aggregated/de-identified data;
- free reporting history is capped at 70 days;
- the platform is not in Verizon's current middle-school allowlist.

Therefore, district approval is required and baseline/midpoint/final evidence must be exported or recorded in Canvas rather than entrusted to year-long vendor retention.

### 2. TypingClub School Edition — fallback

Why it fits:

- structured touch-typing progression, hand-position guidance, tests, teacher scoreboard, WPM, accuracy, practice time, and progress;
- a free School Edition exists.

Why it ranks second:

- free School Edition is limited to three classes and two teachers;
- free activity data retention is 30 days;
- the free tier is not ad-free and advanced reporting is paid;
- three classes may not cover a VILS teacher's full schedule.

### 3. Nitro Type — motivation only

Nitro Type offers a free teacher portal and repeated speed/accuracy racing. Its own positioning distinguishes Typing.com as instruction and Nitro Type as acceleration practice. Use it only after correct technique is established and never as the sole §126.19(c)(12)(D) evidence source.

## Proposed keyboarding pilot

- Curriculum kit: short mini-lessons for posture/hand position and home row, accuracy before speed, progressive keys and punctuation, benchmark habits, and growth reflection.
- Optional full periods: one technique-and-baseline launch and one remediation clinic for students who need more guided practice.
- Cadence menu: teachers may choose twice-weekly 8–12 minute bell ringers, one weekly practice, or short practice bursts within selected units; no single pacing calendar is required.
- Benchmarks: baseline plus at least two later comparable tests and technique spot-checks, scheduled to fit the local course calendar.
- Canvas evidence: benchmark WPM, accuracy, date, technique check, and a short growth reflection; do not store the entire vendor report in a public repository.
- Grading: growth, accuracy, practice consistency, and correct technique—not a single universal WPM cutoff.
- Accessibility: establish an accommodation path before timed testing; do not treat a motor, vision, language, or assistive-technology difference as lack of effort.
