Facilitator's Guide: AI Ethics

Ten lessons that move from documented harms and stakeholder tradeoffs to a debate and a student-written code of ethics.

The unit contract

Days 1 through 7 build the evidence students need. Day 8 is a 100-point debate. Day 9 is a 100-point group code of ethics. Day 10 is the gallery walk and individual synthesis. The topic a group claims on Day 1 should feed its principle on Day 9.

Sequence

  1. Day 1: document one real AI ethics problem, the affected people, and two sources.
  2. Day 2: map direct and indirect stakeholders in a recommendation system, then redesign one rule and name the tradeoff.
  3. Day 3: compare helpful and harmful surveillance using consent, benefit, and the ability to opt out.
  4. Day 4: sort and verify teacher-vetted examples of real and generated content using lateral reading.
  5. Day 5: analyze at least six stakeholders in one AI decision scenario.
  6. Day 6: break a job into tasks, then decide what AI, a human, or both should do.
  7. Day 7: prepare the assigned stakeholder position for the essay-grading debate.
  8. Day 8: conduct the debate and submit evidence, opening, response, and reflection.
  9. Day 9: write at least five evidence-backed principles for creators and users.
  10. Day 10: gallery walk, defend one principle, and explain what changed in your thinking.
  11. Xello: Biases and Career Choices.

Teacher preparation that cannot wait until class

  • Register for Day of AI and download the exact units you plan to use. Current public pages describe the lessons, but the teaching materials require teacher login.
  • Build and preview the Day 4 sorting set. Include a manageable mix of real, AI-generated, altered, and genuinely uncertain examples. Keep the original sources so students can verify them.
  • Choose the Day 5 scenario. The page supports school behavior monitoring, hospital prioritization, sports, or a teacher-approved alternative.
  • Assign the five Day 7 stakeholder roles: student, teacher, parent, technology company, and government or district decision-maker.
  • Prepare a visible timer and voting method for Day 8.
  • Provide poster paper or one shared document per group for Day 9, plus sticky notes or a digital feedback method for Day 10.

Discussion and privacy boundaries

  • Discuss systems and documented cases, not classmates or their families.
  • Students may pass on personal disclosure and still complete the analysis using the supplied scenario.
  • Do not require students to upload a face, personal feed, location history, private messages, or other personal data.
  • A student represents an assigned stakeholder in the debate. The role is not a statement of the student's personal belief.
  • Require a traceable source for factual claims. A broad resource hub is a teacher starting point, not evidence by itself.

Canvas and grading checks

  • Days 1 through 7, Day 9, and Day 10 accept upload or media recording. Their directions now name an uploaded artifact so a comment alone is not mistaken for a submission.
  • Day 8 also accepts text entry, matching the option to type the opening statement.
  • Day 8 and Day 9 each have a 100-point rubric. Review both rubrics with students before Day 7.
  • The current rubrics have no zero-point rating. Their lowest selectable level is 15 points per category; this remains an owner scoring decision.
