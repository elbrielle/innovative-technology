Email Etiquette

Make the message easy to understand, easy to answer, and respectful of the reader's time.

Objective: Write an email that gives the reader enough context and one request they can answer.
Demonstration of learning: Repair four messages, then draft or send one useful school email.

What makes an email useful?

A teacher should not have to guess who you are, what happened, or what you need. A useful school email has six parts:

1 · Subject

Names the topic.

2 · Greeting

Names the reader.

3 · Context

Explains what happened.

4 · Request

Asks for an answerable next step.

5 · Thanks

Closes respectfully.

6 · Your name

Tells the reader who sent it.

Your task

  1. Download the practice sheet.
  2. Repair all four weak messages. Keep the real problem; change the wording that makes the message hard to answer.
  3. Choose one real school need: ask for help, explain an absence, follow up on missing work, or thank someone.
  4. Draft the email. Your teacher will tell you the approved recipient and whether you are sending it or submitting the draft.

Before you send: Check the recipient, subject, attachment, and tone. Do not include a password or private information the reader does not need.

Success check

  • The subject names the topic.
  • The first sentence gives enough context.
  • The request can be answered.
  • The tone is respectful and sounds like you.
  • You checked the recipient and attachment before sending.
Sentence support: I am writing because _____. Could you please _____? Thank you for _____.
