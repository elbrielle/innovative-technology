SW1 · WEEKS 1 AND 2 · TEACHER ONLY

Design Thinking + Superhero Comics

Week 1 gives students one fast pass through design thinking. Week 2 asks them to carry one hero through a cover, biography, smart gadget, logo, and final four-panel solution. The comic is graded once, at the end.

How the two weeks fit

WhenWhat students doWhat they learn
Week 1Run Ready, Set, Design live or through the interactive lesson. Use Five Chair as a second practice round or flex route. Complete the Xello check-in.Design starts with a person and a problem. A first idea is something to test, not something to defend forever.
Week 2Build one paper comic across five connected lessons.Visual choices, character choices, systems, and cause and effect all have to agree well enough for a reader to follow the hero without a spoken explanation.

Why Matchmaker is in Week 1

The S&S names Personality Style and Learning Style in Week 1. Xello lists Matchmaker as phase 1 and Personality Style as phase 2, so the Canvas check-in keeps Matchmaker, Personality Style, and Learning Style in that order. This is a product sequence. It is not a new learning pillar for the Comics project. Review Xello's current assessment sequence.

If students finish Matchmaker here, do not assign the same quiz again in Week 5. Use that later check-in to review results, save careers, or catch up students who were absent.

The comic has one job per page

LessonStudent workLearning piece
1. CoverTitle, hero, and purposeful colour choicesA cover gives a reader the first clues about identity, tone, and audience.
2. BiographyExperience, motivation, ability, challenge, and goalThe same power can lead to different choices. Motivation is what makes the difference visible.
3. GadgetA labelled smart gadget that solves a specific needNeed -> input -> process -> output. Students trace how the gadget receives information, follows a rule, and responds.
4. LogoThree small sketches, one final logo, and short design explanationsA useful logo is simple, recognisable at different sizes, and connected to the hero through meaningful symbols and colours.
5. Smart solutionChallenge, hero choice, gadget action, and visible resultA reader should be able to see the cause and effect. The last panel has to show what changed.

Materials and storage

  • Ready, Set, Design material bags for groups, or the self-paced interactive route.
  • Optional Five Chair materials for a second design round or flex route. The Ready, Set, Design guide has the real facilitator guide, deck, worksheet, checkpoint, and materials list.
  • Three sheets of paper per comic, plus pencils, erasers, and colouring supplies.
  • One teacher sample with the cover and Pages 1 through 4 labelled.
  • A storage plan. Collect the comics between classes so five days of work do not live loose in backpacks.

One grade at the end

Keep Lessons 1 through 4 as checks for progress. The completed comic is one 100-point Major grade: cover and visual identity (10), biography and motivation (15), gadget need/function/input/output (20), logo communication (20), final smart-solution sequence (25), and complete/readable submission (10). Drawing skill is not scored.

Optional video and Animation handoff

The Gabrielle Jordan Williams video is parked as optional enrichment. It is not required for Lesson 4. Teachers who want it may use the original TEDx video or copy the reusable Edpuzzle into their own account.

Animation is a separate, optional five-day to one-and-a-half-week unit after Comics. It may reuse the hero, one action, and the visual identity; Animation adds its own plot diagram, storyboard, production, feedback, and export work. Comics is complete after Lesson 5 whether the teacher runs Animation or moves directly to Graphic Design.

Optional portfolio setup

The portfolio page after Lesson 5 is unpublished and marked optional/flex. It is not part of the five-day Comics sequence. Use it during a flex or catch-up block, assign it in pieces, or leave it parked until a later portfolio week. Do not delay Graphic Design to finish it.

Before publishing for students

  1. Open the Ready, Set, Design interactive lesson in Student View. Use the live presentation route if the SCORM launch is blocked. If you run Five Chair, test its four copy links from the teacher account that will use them.
  2. Test the Ready, Set, Design worksheet copy link and one sample PDF upload.
  3. Confirm students can reach Xello through the district login route. Then publish the Xello check-in when you assign it in Week 1.
  4. Open all five comic decks and leave each lesson's work-time slide projected during independent work.
  5. Test the final comic upload and six-part rubric in Student View.
