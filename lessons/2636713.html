Teach a place in 360°

Lessons 2 and 3 are one project: explore, choose or capture, tag, test, and share.

What stays and what changes

Keep the flat-versus-360 comparison, original videos, camera/library choice, six tags, partner revision, and five-point URL submission. Students start a new 360 scene. They do not reopen the hangout map, follow prescribed mission roles, or connect scenes. Scene connections belong in the branching lessons that follow.

Before class

Set your class photo folder: Create or choose a school-approved folder for this year’s camera photos. Give your students view/download access. In both language panels of Lesson 3, replace the “Class 360° photos” placeholder with a link to that folder. Teacher setup field: [CLASS_PHOTO_FOLDER_URL]. Test using a student account. Do not use another school’s camera folder. The separate Lucero 360 reference library remains an optional image source.

Third image source: Students can also use the existing 360 Media Library (Lucero) from the original module. Students can choose a JPG here instead of using a camera or the ThingLink library, download it, and upload it through Create → 360° image. The same tags, media choices, research, and submission apply. The folder is currently shared read-only with the irvingisd.net domain; verify access on a student account. Do not broaden permissions. Outside Irving, use a teacher-provided library or the ThingLink route. Some files exceed ThingLink’s 25 MB limit; offer a suitable smaller image or a teacher-prepared copy. Keep the original files and attribution intact.

  1. Charge the cameras and check storage. Choose a few permitted school locations, such as the library, courtyard, gym, or an approved classroom/hallway. Confirm room access with the adult responsible. Avoid bathrooms, locker rooms, occupied sensitive spaces, faces, identifying student work, private screens, schedules, and security details.
  2. Rehearse one capture and transfer using the actual camera and student device. The earlier teacher source names Ricoh Theta cameras, but models may differ. Use still-photo mode; keep the camera upright and stable; use a stand/timer if available. Check the whole sphere before students use the image.
  3. For a compatible Theta on a Mac, USB transfer can use Image Capture; Windows can use the device import prompt. Use a data-capable cable. Copy the original JPEG to the student device or a school-approved class folder. Do not disconnect during transfer or delete originals before checking the copy. Test Chromebook/managed-device transfer locally; do not assume it works.
  4. Have a teacher-provided 360 JPEG ready if capture, sign-in, library access, or transfer fails. No personal phones, new personal accounts, or VR headset are required. Confirm students can open the library through their school ThingLink accounts.

Optional camera checkout plan

Print camera plan (English) · Imprimir plan (español)

This one-page plan is optional for teachers, not another graded assignment. If you use it, require students to complete it and give it to you before taking a camera. Check their exact destination, permission contact, six brief tag ideas, and media choices. Approve the location, assign a camera, and write a return time. Keep the plan until the camera is returned.

Release small groups as their plans are approved, within your school’s supervision rules. Set the number of groups out at once and a short capture window. Keep a checkout list with departure and return checks. Waiting students draft tag text, gather permitted media, or research a library location. Students may share a capture; each student submits their own tagged scene unless you explicitly arrange group submissions.

A workable two-period flow

First 50 minutes: 5 minutes for the familiar-place prompt; 10 for the video and real 360 example; 10 for the camera/library and tag demo; 20 for planning, staggered capture or research, and initial tagging; 5 to return cameras and check that every student has a saved scene or accessible image. The goal is not to send the entire class out together.

Next 50 minutes: 5 to reopen work; 5 to model audio with matching text; 25 to finish six tags; 10 for a partner test and one revision; 5 for the URL submission and revision comment. Library-only classes may finish sooner. Keep Lesson 2 as the zero-point introduction and Lesson 3 as the one five-point submission; do not add a second project.

Live demonstration checklist

Route examples and picture steps: In Lesson 3, open the Camera section for The Bear Kitchen and the upload sequence, or the Library section for deep-sea diving and the library sequence. Both languages include real interface captures from ThingLink’s official help demonstration. The old deep-sea viewer was blank during the October 22 browser check; test its direct link on the classroom device before relying on it. It remains the requested library exemplar, not a verified playback claim.

  1. Open Lesson 2. Ask: “What could a new student miss in a regular photo of our library?” Play the existing VR video, then open The Bear Kitchen example. Drag to look around, open a tag, and distinguish looking around from moving between scenes. A 360 photograph is not a navigable 3D model.
  2. Sign in to the class ThingLink account. Demonstrate Create → 360° Image → Library, choose a real identifiable location, and Continue. The library contains both real and AI-generated content; use real photographs for this research task. If the location cannot be identified, choose another.
  3. Demonstrate the camera alternative with one original 360 JPEG. Verify it wraps correctly and can be viewed all around. Required format: equirectangular JPEG, 2:1; current upload limit 25 MB, displayed up to 8192 × 4096. Do not substitute a screenshot or unstitched dual-fisheye image.
  4. Open a Text & Media tag. Give it a useful title. Add information about a visible detail. Model a second content kind and how to credit a borrowed fact or image with its source link. For school facts, confirm with the responsible adult.
  5. Model short audio: in a Text & Media tag, use Upload background audio and record, or add an MP3/M4A through Media files. Read one brief script, play it back, and place the same words in the tag as a transcript. If microphone permission is blocked, students can use text and an image; audio is encouraged, not an extra grading requirement.
  6. Show alt text, readable icons, and captions/transcripts. Save and preview the scene. Use Share → Share link → Copy, then show Lesson 3 → Website URL and the revision comment. Use the class-approved sharing audience; do not require public access.

Keep visible during work

Make: One real 360 location. Six useful tags. At least two content kinds across the tags.
Check: Each tag teaches something. Media opens. Images have descriptions; audio/video has captions or text. A partner can open the link.
Submit: Lesson 3 · Show a Place in 360° → Website URL. Comment: “I changed ___ because my partner ___.”

Project this work checklist or the updated Canvas task while circulating. The former Welcome Mission deck is not linked here because its required visitor roles and map dependency no longer match this lesson. The original videos remain embedded on the student pages.

Feedback, access, and assessment

Use one partner check: “I learned ___. I still want to know ___.” Ask partners to open every tag and identify one unclear detail. Students revise that detail and describe the actual change in the existing submission comment. Keep the five-point assignment and URL-only route. Check the finished scene for accurate location information, six useful tags, two content kinds, readable/accessibility supports, working sharing, and a real revision. Do not score camera access or worksheet completion.

For multilingual learners, preview location, capture, tag, source, and transcript. Keep the bilingual student tabs and sentence starters available. Rehearse narration with a partner; use a home language with an understandable text explanation for the intended audience. Provide an approved image to absent students or those who cannot leave the room. If digital access is unavailable, allow paper tag drafts as preparation, not as evidence of completed digital-tool use.

Alignment and evidence

Objective: Students create an informative 360 scene by choosing media, combining content in tags, and making a revision from partner feedback. Evidence: the working scene plus the actual-revision comment. Camera plans support supervision and planning; they are not an additional required artifact.

Scope: This is practice with observable evidence for media combination and feedback. It does not establish mastery of every file type or the full standards. Data transfer is evidenced only when a student actually performs it; a library-only student does not demonstrate camera transfer. No iCEV assessment coverage is claimed.

Optional Show and Tell: Glow and Grow

Use these postcards with any gallery walk or class show-and-tell. Choose the projects, grouping and viewing order that work for your class. No fixed tour or extra Canvas submission is required.

Print two Glow and Grow postcards on one page

Editable print source (HTML)

  1. Print one sheet per student for feedback on two classmates' projects, or cut the sheets apart and distribute individual cards.
  2. Students view a classmate's work. On the card, they name one specific strength and one useful suggestion. The sentence starters support both responses.
  3. Students fill in To and From, then give each postcard to its creator. Let creators use the feedback to improve their work.
If you use ClassVR headsets

Choose your own approved scenes and prepare the playlist before class. Send/download it to the headsets early and check one scene and its tags before the gallery walk. Use a browser if headsets are unavailable. Web embeds such as YouTube do not play in the ClassVR ThingLink viewer.

Reuse or replace exemplars

  1. Choose work approved for sharing. In your teacher ThingLink account, open the scene and choose … → Clone. Leave the student's original unchanged.
  2. Check the copy's photos, names and tags before sharing. Give it a clear place title and set the approved copy to Anyone with the link.
  3. Choose Share → Share link → Copy. In ClassVR, open Plan → Other libraries → ThingLink, paste the link and add the scene to your playlist.
  4. Download the playlist to your headsets and test it before students arrive.
