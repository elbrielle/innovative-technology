3D MODELING • TEACHER GUIDE

Dream Room: Five-Day Capstone

Students plan from real references, convert measurements, build at a consistent scale, test the model with another person, revise, and submit evidence from Tinkercad.

Daily Learning Contract

Topic: Scale, spatial planning, and iterative modeling

Objective: Students will apply one consistent scale, test whether a room model communicates the planned space, and revise from measurement or peer evidence.

TEKS:
  • §126.19(c)(3)(B) — discuss and implement a design process that includes planning, selecting digital tools to develop, test, and evaluate design limitations, and refining a prototype or model; and
  • §126.19(c)(12)(C) — select and use appropriate platform and tools, including selecting and using software or hardware to transfer data;

Demonstration of learning: Day 1 Slides link; Day 2 conversion worksheet and room shell; final STL and the existing feedback/revision reflection.

Mini-lessons across five days

Day 1: model one reference slide with image and dimensions. Day 2: work one conversion row and keep the same 1:100 scale for every object. Days 3–5: model a room opening with Hole, check furniture sizes, and demonstrate one revision from partner feedback. Leave the current day’s assignment visible during work.

Five-day flow

  1. Day 1: Plan. Choose a room, collect five reference items, and record real dimensions.
  2. Day 2: Scale. Work one conversion row together. Students convert the room and three items by hand before using the calculator.
  3. Day 3: Build the room shell. Floor, walls, door, window, and the first large object.
  4. Day 4: Build and test. Add at least three recognizable items. A partner checks scale, navigation space, and whether the room reads as intended.
  5. Day 5: Revise and submit. Fix one issue from feedback, inspect print-readiness, export the STL, and explain the revision in submission comments.

Teach scale before the worksheet

Use the scale deck for the whole-group mini-lesson. The arithmetic is not the point by itself. Students need to understand that every object must use the same scale or the room stops making visual sense.

Leave-up screen: use the deck's final Convert + Build slide while students work.

Success criteria for the 100-point Major grade

  • Plan and documentation: 15
  • Proportional models: 20
  • Conversion accuracy: 15
  • Consistent and appropriate scale: 15
  • Recognizable room and objects: 20
  • Print-ready construction: 15

Paper access option

For students working without a device: use the pre-filled measurements and drawing page. This practices scale and planning. Keep a later CAD opportunity for evidence of digital modeling.

Support during work

Point students to the word bank beside the reflection. Say the tool name, show it, and model the sentence starter aloud. Students may rehearse in Spanish before writing. Keep the same task and submission; these supports are not extra assignments.

Model the submission: select the complete design, choose Export and .STL, open Downloads, and name the file LastName-FirstName-dream-room.stl. Upload it to the existing assignment and keep the current reflection. Keep the editable design available in Tinkercad. STL contains geometry, not color; inspect the editable model for color and modeling choices. Use the existing Day 1 plan and Day 2 conversion worksheet when scoring those rubric criteria. No additional worksheet or screenshot is required.
