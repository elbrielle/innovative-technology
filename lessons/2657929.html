Daily Learning Contract

Topic: Paths, shapes, and color as visual language

Objective: Students will explain how path, shape, and color choices affect readability and meaning in a digital graphic.

TEKS:
  • §126.19(c)(12)(H) — select and use productivity tools found in spread sheet, word processing, and publication applications to create digital artifacts, including reports, graphs, and charts, with increasing complexity.

Demonstration of learning: Completed Paths, Shapes, and Colors artifact plus a brief explanation of two deliberate choices and their intended effect.

Your name tag idea, rebuilt in Canva as a Pop Art T-shirt graphic. Students recall the Pop Art features from their Lesson 1 name tag, learn the color and lettering vocabulary they need, watch one live Canva demonstration, and build a design with a repeating background, text, a border, and any extra shapes. They export a PNG and upload it to Lesson 3 with one reflection sentence.

Before class

Open Canva through the school ClassLink account and start a blank design. Open Lesson 3 in Canvas so you can show the upload and the Comments box. Students need their Lesson 1 name-tag photo, which is in their Lesson 1 submission.

The assignment stays the same: one Pop Art T-shirt graphic that includes a repetitive background, text, a border around a shape or the text, and extra shapes if the student wants them. Submit one PNG through Lesson 3 and answer the reflection stem in Comments. Points, rubric, and the bilingual tabs are unchanged.

New this revision: a filename. Students name the export LastName-FirstName-Canva-Tshirt.png, matching the Lesson 2 convention. It is on the work slide, in the demo checklist, and in the assignment's export steps in both languages.

Teach the concepts first

SlideWhat you doWhat students notice
1. Design a Pop Art T-shirtPoint to the four features in the POP! artwork: flat bright color, repeated dots, bold lettering, thick outlines.Every feature returns in the assignment.
2. As you enterTwo minutes. Students finish "My name tag uses ___ and ___." Take two or three responses, then say the bridge: same word, same energy, now in Canva.Yesterday's paper choices are today's digital choices.
3. Your missionRead the four required parts from the actual mission box. Point out that the name-tag idea already gives them the word and the feeling.The product is a graphic, not a printed shirt. The four-version level-up is optional.
4. Primary, secondary, tertiaryPoint around the wheel: the three large circles are primaries; the middle circles sit between two primaries; the small circles sit between a primary and a secondary.Pop Art favors primaries beside black outlines, which is why it feels loud.
5. Same design, different colorsAsk which poster shouts and what changed. Vote A or B. Click once to reveal both labels and the explanation.Nothing moved. Only the colors changed, and the mood changed with them.
6. Change a color in CanvaShow the ring: each color circle in the toolbar is one color in the element.Recoloring is a few clicks, one circle at a time.
7. Repeat a shape to make a backgroundWalk through one circle, a row of four, then rows. Name the copy and paste shortcut.Copy and paste builds a pattern quickly. A ready-made dot element is allowed too.
8. Find Pop Art elementsElements, search "pop art," click or drag.Bursts and speech bubbles make borders; dot and stripe graphics make backgrounds.
9. Add a borderCompare the two circles: same fill, border is the only difference. Name the shape route and the text route.A dark border keeps a bright shape readable on a busy background.
10. A font is a style of letteringRead POP! in each style. Name what changes: height, width, weight, curves.Pop Art letters are chunky. Thin or script fonts fight the style.
11. Change the font in CanvaFive steps, with the real Text panel and the font-name control ringed.The font name in the toolbar is where the change happens.

Keep these exchanges short. Slide 2 is the one planned opening discussion and slide 5 is the one point-vote-reveal check. The rest is teacher explanation with the example on screen. A student who reads the two posters differently should point to the visible cue.

PowerPoint presentation mode: slide 5 reveals both labels and the explanation with one click. The next click advances. The reveal uses the same exit-cover mechanism as the approved Lesson 2 deck.

Slide 12: live Canva demonstration

Switch from the deck to Canva. Model the following in one short demonstration, about 6 to 8 minutes. Use your own practice word, not a student's design.

Show thisDemonstrateConnect it to the design idea
A blank workspaceOpen Canva through ClassLink. Start a blank design and name it.The name-tag word or name goes in first, so the design has a job.
BackgroundOpen Elements, search pop art, and add a dot or stripe graphic. Or add a circle from Elements, Shapes, copy and paste it into a row, then paste the row.A repeating background is required. Both routes count.
Burst or bubbleFrom the same search, add a burst or speech bubble and resize it from a corner handle.This can be the border around the word.
ColorsWith the element selected, click one color circle in the toolbar. Pick a primary. Repeat for the other circles.Recolor one circle at a time so students see each change. Keep outlines dark.
Text and fontOpen Text, click Add a text box, type the word. Click the font name in the toolbar, search a display font such as Bangers or Anton, click it. Enlarge with the size box or a corner handle.A font is one style of lettering. Pop Art letters are chunky and high-contrast.
Border on textWith the text selected, open Effects and choose Outline. Set its color and thickness.The border requirement can be met on the text or on a shape.
Border on a shapeSelect a native shape. Open Stroke style, choose the solid line, and raise Stroke weight.Fill is inside; the border marks the edge.
LayersIf the pattern covers the word, open Position, then Layers, and move the background to the back.Front-to-back order decides what shows. Ordinary layering; no Combo app in this lesson.
UndoPress Undo once so students see mistakes are cheap.Try, look, undo.
ExportShare, Download, PNG, Download. Rename the file LastName-FirstName-Canva-Tshirt.png.PNG is the required file type. The name shows whose work it is.
SubmitOpen Lesson 3. Show Start Assignment, the file upload, and the Comments box. Read the stem and word bank.One PNG and one sentence in Comments finish the submission.

Slide 13: explain the task, then leave it up

Return to the work slide and leave it projected for the entire work block. It carries the four required parts, the completion checks, the filename, the export route, the reflection stem and word bank, two examples, and the actual Canvas assignment banner.

During work, check three things in order:

  1. The four parts: a repeating background, a word or name, a border, and optional extra shapes.
  2. The design choice: ask, "Which Pop Art feature did you use, and where is it?" The student should point to the dots, the border, the color, or the lettering and name it with a word-bank term.
  3. The submission: the PNG shows the whole design, the file is named LastName-FirstName-Canva-Tshirt.png, it is uploaded to Lesson 3, and the Comments box holds one sentence: "My design looks like Pop Art because I used ___ to create ___."

Word bank on the slide: bright colors, repeating dots, strong borders, patterns, circles, lines, bold text, repetition.

If a student is stuck, start with the name-tag word alone in a chunky font, then add one background element. The existing level-up is four copies of the finished design with a different color scheme on each, submitted as one panel. Keep it optional.
