Stop Motion Days 4–6 — Film, Edit, and Submit

Daily Learning Contract

Topic: animation production

Student objective: Students will create, edit, and publish a readable stop-motion film with stable, well-lit imagery and sound or titles as appropriate.

Essential TEKS:
  • §126.19(c)(11)(A) — combine various file formats for a specific project or audience; and
Supporting TEKS:
  • §126.19(c)(11)(B) — share and seek feedback on files in various formats, including text, raster and vector graphics, video, and audio files.

Demonstration of learning: Final 30–60 second .mp4 or .mov. The project rubric assesses technical quality, story, creativity, and effort.

Scope: Optional route — demonstrated when assigned

Before class

  • Prepare Stop Motion Studio, charged devices, tripods/books, sets/props, and consistent lighting.
  • Set up a quiet voiceover area and approved sound options.
  • Test final export/upload and keep the rubric visible throughout production.

Five-part lesson flow

  1. Launch: Demonstrate stable framing and a small movement increment.
  2. Model: Capture a short sequence, add sound or title, and export.
  3. Produce: Students build sets and film with daily frame and quality checks.
  4. Review and revise: Peers check stability, lighting, story clarity, and sound; students re-shoot where needed.
  5. Evidence: Students export, upload the final film, and self-score against the rubric.

Check before students leave

The submitted video opens, runs 30–60 seconds, and gives the teacher enough evidence to score story and technical-quality criteria.

Support without lowering the thinking

Provide stable-camera stations and a shot checklist. Students may use simple props, still captions instead of voiceover, and a peer camera assistant; keep story clarity and technical stability as the common standard.
