TEACHER ONLY · COMICS 3

Hero Gadget Design: Solve a Problem

Outcome: Students create Page 2 of their comic with the gadget's name, purpose/function, drawing, problem solved, and at least one input or output. The idea does not have to be possible.

O: Students will understand how clearly defined functions in technology can solve user needs.

D: Students will sketch and label a gadget for their hero, explaining its purpose, inputs, and outputs on a comic page.

A: Gadget -- a small mechanical or electronic device used for a specific function. Input -- a signal or action a device responds to. Output -- what the device produces or does.

Y: What makes a gadget truly “smart” for your hero’s mission?

S: TEKS §127.2(d)(3)(I): define entrepreneurship and identify entrepreneurial opportunities within a field of personal interest. TEKS §127.2(d)(4)(B): identify skills that can be transferable among a variety of careers.

Before class

  • Download the Lesson 3 teacher deck. · Make a Google copy
  • Open the three student-page inspiration dropdowns once before class: Moon Girl, Black Panther's Kimoyo beads, and the Flash's costume ring.
  • Keep the student exemplar and the four required elements visible during work time.

What students need on Page 2

  1. Name: What is the gadget called?
  2. Function: What does it do?
  3. Drawing: What does it look like?
  4. Problem solved: Who or what does it help?
  5. At least one input or output: What does it respond to, or what happens?

Teach input and output with the hand dryer and motion light. Students label at least one input or one output. Do not require a process/if-then chain or a full systems diagram.

One-period route

  1. 5 minutes: Stop & Jot: “Name one problem a superhero might need help solving. What could a gadget do to help?”
  2. 5 minutes: Use the automatic hand dryer and motion-sensor light to model input and output: What does it notice? What happens next?
  3. 5 minutes: Open the three inspiration examples. Ask: What problem does each gadget solve?
  4. 5 minutes: Model one wild idea. Tell students they do not need to decide whether it is possible.
  5. 5 minutes: Students choose a problem connected to their hero.
  6. 18 minutes: Students create Page 2: name, purpose, drawing, problem solved, and at least one input or output.
  7. 5 minutes: Partner explanation using the sentence stem.
  8. 2 minutes: Exit check: name the problem and explain how the gadget helps.

Teacher model

Think aloud: “My hero wants people to feel safe walking home. The problem is that a dark sidewalk can feel unsafe. My gadget is the Pocket Sun. Its function is to float ahead and light the path. I will draw the light, the person, and the safe path.”

Say: “A floating pocket sun may not be possible. That is fine today. We are practicing invention and problem solving.”

Add one technology label: “Its input could be darkness. Its output could be light. A student only needs to mention one of those on the page.”

Supports

  • Planning stem: My gadget is called ____. It helps ____ by ____.
  • Explanation stem: It solves the problem of ____ because ____.
  • Spanish: Mi gadget se llama ____. Ayuda a ____ al ____.
  • Let students explain orally while a partner or teacher helps add labels.
  • Accept a diagram, one large drawing, or comic panels as long as all four requirements are communicated.

Check before students leave

  • The gadget has a memorable name.
  • The function is understandable.
  • The drawing makes the idea visible.
  • The page names or clearly shows the problem being solved.
  • The student mentions or labels at least one input or output.
How to teach input and output without overloading the page

Ask two questions: “What makes it start or what does it receive?” and “What happens or comes out?” Teach both concepts, then let students label at least one input or one output. Skip process rules unless a student chooses the extension.

Handoff to Lesson 4: The hero and gadget now have a purpose. The next lesson turns that identity into a recognizable logo.
