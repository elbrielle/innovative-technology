3D Modeling Lesson 2 — Coin, Solids + Holes

Model the key skill, leave the student task visible, and check the existing submission.

Daily Learning Contract

Topic: Constructive solid geometry

Objective: Students will explain how solids, holes, alignment, and grouping combine to create precise 3D form.

TEKS:
  • §126.19(c)(12)(C) — select and use appropriate platform and tools, including selecting and using software or hardware to transfer data;

Demonstration of learning: STL file named LastName-FirstName-coin.stl and the existing 2–3 sentence comparison with drawing on paper.

Before students begin

  • Open the student task in Student View and test the exact sign-in, link, file, or submission path students will use.
  • Prepare only the listed existing materials; do not require a personal account or a new unlisted product.
  • Keep the current student page available as the accessible and absence route.

8–10 minutes: model one coin

Open your paper coin plan and Tinkercad. Add a cylinder, resize it, and compare width, length, and height. Add one raised symbol. Turn a second shape into a Hole, align it, and Group to cut a detail. Demonstrate Undo, then select the complete coin and choose Export → .STL. Open Downloads, rename the file LastName-FirstName-coin.stl, and upload it to the assignment. Explain that STL holds the 3D surface, not its colors; a slicer prepares it for a specific printer. Keep the editable original in Tinkercad. Students then build from their own sketch. Leave the assignment visible.

Lesson flow

  1. 1. Launch: Students will use Tinkercad solids, holes, and alignment to model a coin.
  2. Model: follow the checklist above, then have students try the same controls on their own design.
  3. 3. Guide: Complete the first decision or step together; conference before students commit to a direction.
  4. 4. Build: Students work from the assignment while you check their models.
  5. 5. Check + next step: Verify this evidence before students leave: Current 3D coin/Tinkercad artifact and required submission evidence.

Scaffolds and teacher decisions

Keep the student lesson's bilingual tabs, word bank, models, and current support route in view while students work. Reduce the number of examples or provide a partially completed planning frame before reducing the evidence. If a student needs an alternate format, preserve the same named evidence and use the current rubric or teacher judgment rather than adding a hidden requirement.

Close the loop

Check that the submitted STL opens, includes the full coin, and uses LastName-FirstName-coin.stl. Read the existing reflection. Review any automated grading recommendation before recording a grade. Note who needs help with the next modeling skill.

Support during work

Point students to the word bank beside the reflection. Say the tool name, show it, and model the sentence starter aloud. Students may rehearse in Spanish before writing. Keep the same task and submission; these supports are not extra assignments.
