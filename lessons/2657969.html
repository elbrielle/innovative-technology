AR Lesson 4 — Interactive AR Game Design

Daily Learning Contract

Topic: physics-based AR games

Student objective: Students will create a playable AR game with a clear goal, physics, and at least ten purposeful parts.

Essential TEKS:
  • §126.19(c)(1)(E) — develop, compare, and improve algorithms for a specific task to solve a problem;
Supporting TEKS:
  • §126.19(c)(12)(F) — apply appropriate troubleshooting techniques and seek technical assistance as needed;

Demonstration of learning: Lesson 4 Canvas link or file upload showing a pinball game, marble maze, or approved arcade idea with physics, ten or more parts, and a clear goal. Space Pinball remains the target example.

Scope: Demonstrated

Before class

  • Open the Space Pinball or comparable example.
  • Project the required Physics.realTime = true; line for the script editor.
  • Prepare Merge Cubes, Delightex/CoBlocks, and a game-design checklist.

Five-part lesson flow

  1. Launch: Play or inspect the example and name the goal, player action, rule, and feedback.
  2. Model: Add the real-time physics line and test one moving object.
  3. Plan: Students choose pinball, maze, or an approved idea and list ten parts plus a win condition.
  4. Build and playtest: Students build, play in pairs, and troubleshoot physics or collision failures.
  5. Evidence: Students submit working evidence and verify goal, physics, parts, and playability.

Check before students leave

The game runs when tested, its goal is understandable without explanation, and the player can observe the physics behavior.

Support without lowering the thinking

Give students labeled boxes for goal, player action, rule, and feedback. Let a student explain a plan orally while a peer records labels; keep the physics troubleshooting line visible for everyone.
