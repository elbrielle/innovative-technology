Facilitator’s Guide: SPIKE PRIME Overview

Everything you need to run Invention Squad week.

Instructional Alignment

Topic: Engineering test data

Student objective: Students will Define a problem, prototype, run comparable tests, analyze results, and recommend a design

Essential TEKS:
  • §126.19(c)(3)(B) — discuss and implement a design process that includes planning, selecting digital tools to develop, test, and evaluate design limitations, and refining a prototype or model; and
  • §126.19(c)(6) — The student uses digital tools to transform data, make inferences, and predictions. The student is expected to use digital tools in order to transform data, analyze trends, and predict possibilities and develop steps for the creation of an innovative process or product.
Supporting TEKS:

None claimed for this module-level record.

Demonstration of learning: problem statement, prototype log, data table, and recommendation.

Alignment status: D

📅 5 days + an optional extension🛠️ LEGO SPIKE Prime kits📦 Teams of 2–3

The unit at a glance

Invention Squad — Engineering Design Week: define the problem, prototype, then test
Day 1: Help! (Find the Problem)Teams build Kiki the dog, run two programs and listen, then brainstorm for sixty seconds, trade lists with another team, and write a real problem statement plus 2–3 success criteria.~45 min

Closing

Materials + prep

  • Charge every SPIKE Prime hub before the week starts, not the morning of Day 1. A dead hub costs a team the whole period.
  • Inventory the kits. Day 1 needs the Color Sensor for Kiki; Days 4–5 need enough parts for a hand controller plus two different grabbers per team at the same time. A missing sensor is invisible until a team is halfway into the build.
  • The SPIKE App installed on at least one device per team for Days 1, 4 and 5, and the LEGO SPIKE website reachable for the Hopper Race build.
  • A 50 cm track marked on a table or the floor, and a timer per team, for Days 2–3.
  • Test objects for Super Cleanup that differ on purpose: a crumpled paper ball, an empty plastic bottle, an apple or something about that heavy, and one small object like a marker.
  • The Prototype Log worksheet for Hopper Race opens in Google Docs — open it yourself once and confirm it hands back.
  • Xello through ClassLink for the check-in. Day 1, Days 4–5, the Extension and the check-in all arrive unpublished; publish them as you reach them.

Watch-fors

  • Teams of 2–3, and make them split the job. Every one of these lessons says it: one person hunts parts while one builds, then trade. Left alone, one student builds for the whole week and the other two watch.
  • The Extension is a stretch, not a requirement. It is a bonus build for teams who finish Super Cleanup and turn it in. Do not plan the week around it, and do not hold the class for it.
  • Criteria before testing, every time. Days 1, 4 and 5 all hinge on students writing down what counts as success before they start. If they test first and decide after, they will simply crown whichever design they already liked.
  • Change one thing at a time. Hopper Race falls apart if a team changes three things between runs, because then no time means anything. Say it on Day 2 and say it again on Day 3.

Grading

Days 2–3: Hopper Race is the unit’s Major grade, at 100 points, and the Prototype Log that comes back through Google Docs is what you score. Day 1, Days 4–5 and the Extension are Minor grades worth 5 points each, submitted as a file upload or media recording, and the Xello Check-in is another Minor 5. Required work comes to 115 points, plus 5 more if a team does the Extension.
