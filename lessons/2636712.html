Lessons 1 and 1.5: Design and Tag Your Hangout

Students design an imaginary hangout, draw its map, then upload that image to ThingLink and tag at least three different zones and use at least two kinds of content across the tags. Lesson 1 is drawing and a webcam-photo submission. Lesson 1.5 starts by downloading that saved photo, then adding interactive tags. No superhero character, visitor mission, or assigned Arrive/Find/Help route is required.

Daily Learning Contract

Topic: Immersive media introduction, map design and interactive tags

Objective: Students design a readable map, add useful interactive tags, test them with a partner, and improve one detail.

TEKS:
  • §126.19(c)(3)(B) — discuss and implement a design process that includes planning, selecting digital tools to develop, test, and evaluate design limitations, and refining a prototype or model; and
  • §126.19(c)(11)(A) — combine various file formats for a specific project or audience; and
  • §126.19(c)(11)(B) — share and seek feedback on files in various formats, including text, raster and vector graphics, video, and audio files.
  • §126.19(c)(12)(C) — select and use appropriate platform and tools, including selecting and using software or hardware to transfer data;

Demonstration of learning: Lesson 1: a readable photo of a student-created map with at least three labeled areas. Lesson 1.5: three working tags on different zones using at least two kinds of content, an observed partner-tested improvement, and a viewer link submitted in Canvas.

Alignment scope: Practice and task-level evidence, not a claim of full standard mastery. For 11(A), treat image tagging as introductory or partial evidence unless the student actually combines imported file formats. Typed tag text alone does not prove multiple file formats. Optional audio/video does not establish evidence in every format listed in 11(B). No iCEV assessment coverage is claimed.

Student exemplars

Student map 1 (public teacher clone) · Student map 2

Introduce these as last year’s student work. Say: “They designed superhero headquarters. You will design your own hangout. We are borrowing the map-and-tag idea, not the theme.” Do not imply that every detail in an old exemplar is a current requirement.

Lesson 1: Draw and submit the map (50-minute model)

  1. 3 minutes: Slide 1 bell ringer. Students jot three things they would like to DO in a hangout on the edge or back of their drawing paper, circle a favorite, then share briefly with a partner. Accept quick symbols. Use the idea bank and “I’d want a place to ___ because ___.” No extra submission.
  2. 8 minutes: Slides 2–5 introduce immersive media. Show the real classroom headset photo, briefly open the 360 park, compare VR and AR, then preview the course projects. Use the short teaching notes below. Keep this a guided preview, not account setup or a separate practice assignment.
  3. 2 minutes: Slide 6 connects the brainstorm to drawing. Model how making art could become an art corner with a table and supply shelf. Students choose areas that fit their own interests.
  4. 5 minutes: Slides 7–9 show real student maps. Point to labels, details and connections. On slide 8, ask what the pool tag might explain. Advance once to slide 9 to reveal it. The earlier superhero theme is not required.
  5. 5 minutes: Model a rough three-area layout on paper or in a familiar drawing tool. Show how labels, doors or paths make connections clear. Keep the model achievable today.
  6. 22 minutes: Leave slide 10 projected. Students name their hangout, draw at least three connected areas, label them and add recognizable details. Conference while they draw.
  7. 5 minutes: Model Start Assignment, File Upload, Use Webcam, Take Photo, Use This Photo and Submit Assignment in Lesson 1. Photograph only the upright, readable map. Students open the submitted image to confirm it saved. If webcam capture is blocked, upload a JPG/PNG from a school camera tool. Keep the paper until the backup is checked.

Immersive-media introduction: slides 2–5

  • Immersion: a sense of being present in a place or experience. Interaction means acting on something. The two can overlap. A clickable map is our starting point for interactive design, not automatically a fully immersive experience.
  • 360 scene: open the Westpark tour, click Play, drag to look around and choose one path. Looking around a 360 photograph changes the direction of the view from a camera position. A path link can load another scene; it is not free movement through a photograph.
  • VR and AR: VR replaces the view with a digital environment. AR adds digital content to the view of the real world. Ask one quick check: “Which example still shows the real world?” Point to the city behind the digital figure in the source illustration.
  • Coming projects: interactive maps, 360 images and scenes, branching stories, then later VR and AR creation. A branching story follows different paths based on the reader’s choices. Delightex is the current name for CoSpaces Edu. Sand Land is a later course example, not a new planet assignment today. Its browser preview is not a demonstration of camera-view AR.

Keep the preview bounded: Do not require personal phones, headset setup, app installs or new accounts today. Use screenshots if a live viewer is blocked and describe what the interactive version does. These concepts are introduced here; the map photo alone does not demonstrate VR or AR tool mastery. Later module timing and AR/VR order remain the teacher’s decision.

If account setup needs 10–15 minutes, complete it in place of part of work time and allow the unfinished map to continue next class. Do not quietly add a separate practice project. For a 60-minute period, add the extra time to drawing. Do not shorten the photo-backup check to fit the introduction.

Lesson 1.5: Download and tag the map (50-minute model)

  1. 10 minutes: Use slide 11. Students click Open ThingLink on the Canvas homepage, choose Login with Google and their school account, then Use invite code. Class code: your teacher’s code. Enter it and click Join. Skip joining if already in the correct organization. Choose Student if asked. Do not create duplicate personal accounts. Then retrieve the saved Lesson 1 image from the submission and locate it in Downloads.
  2. 10 minutes: Slide 12 introduces the tag choices. Use slide 13 for one complete demo: upload the map, create a Text & media tag with a name, short explanation and relevant image, preview, then save. Explain that text + image meets the two-content-types minimum.
  3. 20 minutes: Leave slide 15 projected. Students tag at least three different zones and use at least two kinds of content across their tags. Text, image, audio and video are content types. Two different tag buttons are not required. The background map image does not count as an image inside a tag.
  4. 5 minutes: Use slide 14, then return to slide 15. Partners open every tag. Each creator improves one unclear detail and tests again.
  5. 5 minutes: Copy and test the viewer link, then submit it in Lesson 1.5. Preserve this submission check if setup takes longer. Continue unfinished tagging next class rather than adding a separate practice project.

Live-demo checklist

  1. Open the actual school ThingLink route. Show how to return to existing work. Account menus may differ; test the class account before projecting.
  2. Choose Create and the image upload option. Select the image downloaded from the Lesson 1 submission. Name the scene so the student can find it again.
  3. Open Edit if needed, then Add tag. Select a text-and-media tag. Place it on one area without covering its label.
  4. Enter the area’s name. Model a short explanation: “Art corner: Make posters and paint here. The shelf holds supplies.” Preview it before duplicating the process.
  5. Demonstrate a relevant image with the tag explanation, so students see two kinds of content together. Model source credit and an image description. Audio/video are other choices, with captions or a short transcript. Two kinds across all tags is the minimum, not two in each tag.
  6. Move the tag and select an icon with clear contrast. Add two more tags on the remaining areas. Finish editing and open each tag as a viewer.
  7. Return to the project list and reopen the scene. Confirm that the work is saved and teach the recovery route before students close the tab.
  8. Open Share, select the school-approved access setting, and copy the viewing link rather than the editor address. Test it from the partner’s account. Public sharing is not required by this lesson.
  9. Open the Canvas assignment Lesson 1.5 · Tag Your Hangout Map, choose the website URL submission, paste the link, and submit. Do not submit a static PNG instead of the interactive map.

The button names above follow the linked ThingLink image guide. Live student-account behavior and the exact group route still require classroom verification. The original tutorial remains linked on the student page for replay or absence support.

Feedback and completion

Ask the partner to name the areas and explain what happens in each after opening the tags. Use: “I understood ___. I wanted to know ___.” The creator can explain: “I changed ___ so ___.” Observe the revision during conferences; do not add a second worksheet or extra graded reflection.

Lesson 1.5 keeps the existing 5 points: 1 for the named place and readable layout; 1 for at least three labeled zones; 1 for working tags on those zones using at least two kinds of content, with appropriate credit and accessible alternatives; 1 for partner testing and an improvement; 1 for a working submitted viewer link.

Lesson 1 photo checkpoint: Complete/incomplete, 0 points, excluded from the final grade. It preserves the image without adding weight to the existing project. File Upload has no extension restriction so Canvas can offer Use Webcam. Check for a readable image rather than a video or PDF.

Access and recovery

For a student without device access, arrange a supervised school-device photo upload for Lesson 1 and keep the paper until the image is saved. Use the same map. Number three areas and write the matching tag text on the back or on the same sheet. A partner reads each numbered description. This demonstrates planning and explanation, not independent ThingLink tool mastery. Resume the digital build when access returns.

If an image uploads sideways or blurry, retake or rotate the photo before uploading. If a link is blocked, test the audience setting and school account rather than making student work public. A missing 360 image must not prevent Lesson 1 drawing; later 360 work will need a teacher-selected scene or an explicit adaptation, not a promise that the exact invented room exists in the library.
