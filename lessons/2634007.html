TEACHER ONLY · COMICS LESSON 5

Smart Solution in Action + Final Comic Submission

Lesson outcome: Students complete Page 4 as a readable sequence showing a challenge, the hero's choice, the gadget in action, and a visible result. They then submit the cover and Pages 1 through 4 as the 100-point unit assessment.

Projection deck: Open Smart Solutions Lesson 5: Smart Solution in Action. The Canvas assignment is the accessible student and absence route.

Before class

  • Open the final assignment and confirm the revised 100-point whole-comic rubric is attached.
  • Set out the comics, pencils, and coloring supplies. Students continue with the same hero, gadget, and visual identity from Pages 1 through 3.
  • Decide whether students will use one of the three provided sustainability prompts or choose another teacher-approved access, safety, communication, or community problem.
  • Choose a submission route: separate photos of the cover and four pages, or one slow, readable video when separate photos create an access barrier.
  • Test the assignment preview with a student account before class.

Teaching sequence

  1. Bellringer and vocabulary: Students study the three real problem images, name what they notice, and identify who or what is affected.
  2. Challenge and solution: Define a challenge as a problem or condition that affects a person, group, place, or resource. Define a solution as an idea or action that improves at least part of the situation.
  3. Sustainability choice: Introduce water, energy, and shared spaces as possible contexts for this lesson. Sustainability is not a required focus for the rest of the course.
  4. Teacher model: Read the VilBot example from first panel to last. Point to the challenge, hero's choice, gadget action, and visible result.
  5. Plan: Students write one short note for each of the four parts before drawing. Check the order and result before they add details.
  6. Build and partner test: The partner reads first while the creator stays quiet. If the reader cannot point to one of the four parts, the creator adds a visual, label, caption, arrow, or speech bubble.
  7. Submit: Students photograph or record the whole comic, open every preview, and submit.

Pacing choices

One-period route: Move from the challenge choices to the VilBot model, plan four panels, work, run a short partner check, and photograph at the end. Use this only when students already have complete Pages 1 through 3.

Two-period route: On Day 1, teach through planning and begin the page. On Day 2, reopen with the four-part trace, finish and partner-test the sequence, then complete the whole-comic submission.

What to look for while students work

  • The challenge affects someone or something specific.
  • The hero makes a choice that fits the motivation developed on Page 1.
  • The gadget action still uses the input, process or rule, and output established on Page 2.
  • The hero and gadget still carry the visual identity developed on Page 3.
  • The final panel is visibly different from the first. A paragraph should not be needed to explain what changed.

Language and access support

  • Keep the work-time slide projected. It includes the exact rubric focus, bilingual word bank, and complete English and Spanish sentence stems.
  • Let students plan with short notes, sketches, labels, arrows, or speech bubbles. Drawing skill and coloring are not scored.
  • For students who freeze at a blank page, start with the result panel and work backward: What caused this result? What did the hero notice? What challenge started the scene?
  • Allow oral rehearsal before writing and accept the final exit response orally when that preserves the same evidence.

Whole-comic rubric

  • Cover and visual identity: 10 points
  • Biography and motivation: 15 points
  • Gadget need, function, input, and output: 20 points
  • Logo communication: 20 points
  • Final smart-solution sequence: 25 points
  • Complete and readable submission: 10 points

Optional Animation handoff

The comic is complete after Lesson 5. If you choose the optional Animation unit, students carry forward the hero, one action from Page 4, and the visual identity they already built. Animation then runs as its own five-day to one-and-a-half-week design cycle. If you move directly to Graphic Design, nothing is missing from the comic unit.
