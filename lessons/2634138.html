Facilitator's Guide: Machine Learning Week

Five lessons about how models learn from examples, how training choices shape results, and how to test a model instead of trusting its confidence score.

Where this week sits

The S&S places this after the introduction to AI and before the two-week AI Ethics module. Students move from defining machine learning to training, bias, classification, computer vision, and one ethical question they carry forward.

Sequence and evidence

  1. Day 1: Code.org introduction, three examples from daily life, and a journal response.
  2. Day 2: two-label Teachable Machine model, balanced and unbalanced tests, and confidence-score screenshot.
  3. Day 3: Code.org bias activity, a deliberately limited model, a failure log, and one specific data fix.
  4. Day 4: AI for Oceans classification, accuracy before and after more training data, and a real-world comparison.
  5. Day 5: computer vision, model testing, four-question synthesis, and one ethical concern for the next unit.
  6. Xello: Make Plans and upload one completed plan screenshot.

Source and account check

  • Open the Code.org How AI Works lessons and AI for Oceans from a student account before the week begins.
  • Test Teachable Machine camera access on district Chromebooks. It does not require a login, but the browser must allow the camera.
  • Day of AI now places downloadable curriculum behind teacher registration. Download or assign the exact material before class; do not send students to search the resource hub.
  • Confirm Xello access through ClassLink before the final check-in.

Materials and setup

  • One Chromebook per student or pair, with a working camera for Days 2, 3, and 5.
  • Headphones for the short videos.
  • A journal or digital note page students can photograph or export for submission.
  • Pairs for Teachable Machine testing. Students should test each other's models rather than only testing the same person and background used for training.
  • A plan for students who cannot or should not use the camera. Use teacher-provided image sets rather than requiring a personal image.

What to check

  • More confidence does not automatically mean a correct prediction.
  • A model can work for its training conditions and fail under new lighting, distance, background, or person.
  • When students propose a bias fix, require the exact data they would add or rebalance.
  • Assignment submissions need an uploaded artifact as well as the reflection comment because the five Canvas assignments accept upload or media recording, not text entry.

Current sources: Code.org How AI Works Lesson 1 · Teachable Machine · Day of AI: AI Foundations for Middle Grades · AI for Oceans
