Facilitator’s Guide: Image Classification (Picture This)

Train an AI to see, then give it a job: three Canvas items across a five-day week.

📅 5 days, 3 items🔧 Teachable Machine · RAISE Playground🏆 100 points as built

Materials + prep

  • Webcams working and camera permission allowed. Teachable Machine asks for the camera the moment students start recording samples. A device policy that blocks it will stop Day 1 dead, so test on a student Chromebook, not yours.
  • The RAISE Playground template opened once before Friday. It loads a project file from a link, and it is slow the first time. Have it up on the projector when the period starts.
  • The planning doc from the Days 4 and 5 assignment. Each student’s copy is already attached. They open it, they do not make a copy.
  • Space for students to move. The lean-left / lean-right models need students to actually move in front of the camera, and 30 photos per class means everybody is waving at once.
  • Headphones for the Day 1 walkthrough video.

Watch-fors

  • Three Canvas items cover five days. Day 1 is one item, Days 4 and 5 share one, and the turn-in is the third. Days 2 and 3 have no Canvas item. That is where the Day 1 “try to break it” work gets discussed and extended. Nothing is missing from the module. It just is not written down, so plan those two periods yourself.
  • Two items are called “Coding with Models.” The one in square brackets is the turn-in. On Friday, point at it by name or half the class will upload into the wrong assignment.
  • Class names have to match, letter for letter. The Playground code reads “when model detects ___” against the exact strings from Teachable Machine. A capital letter or a stray space is the single most common Friday failure, and it looks like broken code.
  • The bias check happens twice, on purpose. Thursday step 3 has partners test each other’s models, and Friday ends with “did my AI fail anyone?” That question is the whole point of the week, and Machine Learning Week and AI Ethics pick it up later.
  • The .sb3 file only exists if they save it. The Playground does not keep their work. Give the last five minutes of Friday to File → Save to your computer and upload, not to one more round of testing.

Grading

[Project Turn In] Coding with Models is the week’s Major grade, at 100 points, and it takes the .sb3 file upload. The two lessons that feed it are 0 points: Intro to Image Classification takes a website URL plus a reflection in the comments, and Coding with Models runs through Google Assignments with the planning doc. Read both before you score the turn-in, because that is where the work happened.
