Individual · Glider, portfolio, and on-site rebuild

Flight

You will design a glider that stays in the air as long as possible.

You design and test gliders.You fly your prepared glider.You rebuild and fly if you advance.

What you make or do

  • Build and test gliders, then bring a completed competition glider.
  • Prepare a printed portfolio with a full-size metric technical drawing, photos and flight logs for two test gliders, a detailed illustration, and a technical review of a flight log.

This year’s challenge

No separate annual theme is posted. The challenge is to build a rule-compliant glider that stays aloft after a catapult launch.

How the competition works

Before the event

Build and test your glider designs and complete the portfolio. Pack the approved tools and material blanks needed to rebuild your glider if you advance, along with safety eyewear.

First round

Fly your prepared glider four times using TSA's catapult. The three best flight times determine who advances; there is no extra trim time in this round.

If you advance

Rebuild a glider from your technical drawing in 45 minutes, then receive ten minutes for trim flights. Complete four official flights, and have your portfolio evaluated.

Know before you choose: You need both a finished glider and the ability to reconstruct it on site. A completed technical drawing is required to enter the semifinal construction round.

How your work is scored

Flight performance selects semifinalists. The semifinal score includes the documentation portfolio and points for flight performance; the current rubric calls the detail visual a Detail Illustration.
