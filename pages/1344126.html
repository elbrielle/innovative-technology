System Control Technology

Your team builds and programs a model that carries out a process automatically.

Interpreting the on-site problemBuilding and programming a modelDemonstrating the solution

What you make or do

  • Build and program a computer-controlled mechanical model that solves the on-site problem.
  • Complete an Inventor's Log explaining the problem, your solution, and the instructions judges need to operate the model.
  • Demonstrate the model and explain the program and mechanical features to judges, answering their questions.

This year’s challenge

There is no separate annual theme listed. Teams receive an on-site problem, usually involving an industrial process. The recycling-system example in the guide is a practice example.

How the competition works

Before the event

Choose a team captain and prepare the required computer, software, and materials kit. Competition programs must be written entirely on site.

First round

After captain setup, use fifteen minutes to analyze the problem and begin the Inventor's Log. Your team then has two and a half hours to build, program, test, and document the solution before the demonstration and questions.

How the event finishes

No separate advancing round is described in the student procedure. All teams demonstrate their model and take part in the interview as part of the same event.

Know before you choose: The current rule update corrects the problem-analysis period to fifteen minutes. You must write the program on site and leave clear instructions so judges can operate and reset the model.

How your work is scored

Judges assess the Inventor's Log, how effectively and dependably the model solves the problem, the program's structure and efficiency, and the demonstration/interview.
