Drone Challenge (UAV)

You and a partner will fly a drone through humanitarian-aid missions.

What you make or do

  • Prepare and test a commercially purchased or team-built drone that meets the event specifications.
  • Create a printed portfolio explaining problems related to humanitarian aid and rescue, up to three ways drones could help, your research sources, and work log.

This year’s challenge

Operation Lifeline focuses on humanitarian aid after a disaster. Missions involve precision landings, moving a relief marker, containing lightweight debris, and navigating damaged access routes.

How the competition works

Before the event

Prepare the drone, practice flying the current missions, and complete the research portfolio. Bring the drone, portfolio, safety glasses, and safety vests.

First round

After orientation and safety checks, complete three one-minute manual flight attempts. Both teammates must pilot at least once; the highest-ranking flight entries then have their portfolios judged.

If you advance

Semifinalist teams take part in a ten-minute interview about drone capabilities and their proposed uses.

Know before you choose: One teammate pilots while the other spots. The official mission packet contains conflicting point values, so confirm scoring with your teacher before making a practice score sheet.

How your work is scored

Flight performance, the research portfolio, and the semifinal interview contribute to the event. Read the current mission packet with the guide because annual scoring details differ.
